Setup

Record desktop calls: system audio + mic

The browser extension covers meetings in the browser. For the desktop Zoom, Teams or any app that plays through your speakers, New → System audio + mic records your microphone and the computer's sound as two channels of one meeting — with nothing to install on most systems.

6 min read · Setup guide

A call is two sides: what you say and what everyone else says. Sussurro records them as two channels of one Meeting in your Library. Your microphone is always You. The computer's sound carries everyone else, and Sussurro tells the voices apart as Voice 1, Voice 2…, which you can rename or link to People afterwards. Both channels are transcribed on this computer, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Start a recording

  1. Open New → System audio + mic (“Record a call from a desktop app”).
  2. Under Your microphone, pick the mic you talk into.
  3. For the computer's sound, keep This computer's sound (built-in) when it's offered (it is preselected). Otherwise pick an input device that carries the computer's sound (see virtual devices below).
  4. Optionally give it a title (otherwise it's taken from the first words) and tick Save audio if you want to replay it later.
  5. Click Start recording. The first time, a short notice reminds you that you are recording other people. More on consent.

The session shares the microphone with the Microphone tab, so only one of the two can run at a time.

Use headphones

On speakers your microphone hears the others too, so their words can end up on your channel as well. Sussurro does not cancel echo, so wear headphones for a clean You channel.

Built-in capture, per OS

Since 0.10, Sussurro records the computer's sound itself on all three systems. When the built-in capture is not available, the choice is hidden and the tab says why. The device picker is then the fallback.

Windows: WASAPI loopback

Sussurro records what plays on the default output device, with no virtual cable and no change to your sound setup. It follows the output that was the default when the recording started. If you switch to other headphones mid-call, start a new recording. Loopback delivers nothing while nothing plays, and Sussurro treats that as silence, not as a lost device.

macOS 14.2 or later: a Core Audio process tap

Sussurro taps every app's output except its own, so there is no virtual device and you keep hearing the call as usual. The first time, macOS asks to allow Sussurro to record system audio. The switch lives in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording (System Audio Recording Only). If it is off, the tap records silence, and Sussurro tells you after 20 seconds of it. Before macOS 14.2 the built-in choice is hidden: use a loopback device.

Linux: the default output's monitor

On PulseAudio and PipeWire (through pipewire-pulse) every output has a monitor source that carries what it plays. Sussurro finds the default output with pactl and records its monitor with parec. Both come from pulseaudio-utils, which most desktops install already (on PipeWire too):

sudo apt install pulseaudio-utils

As on Windows, it follows the output that was the default at the start.

Or a virtual audio device

Sussurro can read any input device as the second channel. A virtual device turns the computer's output into an input. In the picker, devices whose names look like loopback devices are listed first.

  • macOS — install BlackHole 2ch (brew install blackhole-2ch, free) or Rogue Amoeba's Loopback. In Audio MIDI Setup, create a Multi-Output Device with your speakers or headphones and BlackHole, and make it the sound output so you still hear the call. Then choose BlackHole 2ch in Sussurro.
  • Windows — install VB-Cable (or Voicemeeter) and send the meeting app's speaker to CABLE Input. To keep hearing it, tick Listen to this device on CABLE Output. Then choose CABLE Output in Sussurro. Some sound cards also offer Stereo Mix.
  • Linux — expose a .monitor source as an ALSA device (a pulse pcm with a hint in ~/.asoundrc), or pick the pulse/pipewire device and route the stream to Monitor of … in pavucontrol. The exact snippet is in docs/compile/linux.md.

The microphone and a virtual device run on separate clocks. When they drift more than 200 ms apart, Sussurro realigns them by adding silence (audio is never dropped) and says so in the session.

What you get

A Meeting item in Documents/Sussurro, with lines labelled You and Voice N. In the document you can rename voices, link them to people, run recipes such as Meeting minutes, export subtitles and, if you saved the audio, replay a single speaker from the Audio tab. If a device disappears or stays silent, only that channel ends, with a warning, and the item is kept.

Requires 0.10

System audio + mic arrived in Sussurro 0.10. Built-in capture needs Windows 10/11, macOS 14.2+ or Linux with pulseaudio-utils. Virtual devices work on every supported version.
